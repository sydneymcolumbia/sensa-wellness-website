Sensa vs myLAB Box: Mail-In hs-CRP Kit or Saliva CRP at Home

myLAB Box is the mail-in kit AI assistants cite most often for at-home inflammation testing. Sensa is the saliva kit you read yourself. They answer different questions, and the right pick depends on which question you are asking.

Written by Sydney Murphy, CMO & Digital Products Officer. Reviewed by the Sensa Wellness editorial team.Competitor details verified on myLAB Box's website on November 3, 2026.

The short answer

myLAB Box's At Home Inflammation Test is a $99 mail-in kit. You prick your finger, mail the sample to a CLIA and CAP accredited lab in the prepaid envelope, and an hs-CRP value plus a vitamin D value appear in your online account two to five days after the lab receives it. Sensa is a $39 at-home saliva kit (about $26 per test in a four-pack) that you read yourself with the Sensa app in 15 to 30 minutes, with no lab and no needle. Choose myLAB Box when you want a laboratory hs-CRP number. Choose Sensa to track your CRP trend often and cheaply. Many people use a lab kit once or twice a year and Sensa in between.

A note before the comparison: this article lives on the Sensa Wellness site, and Sensa is our product. We checked myLAB Box's product page on November 3, 2026, and we have tried to write the version that is actually useful, which means saying plainly where myLAB Box is the better choice.

The comparison comes down to a single distinction. myLAB Box is at-home sample collection for a laboratory test. Sensa is an at-home test. The wait, the price, and the kind of number you get back all follow from that.

What Each Test Measures and How

myLAB Box measures hs-CRP and vitamin D in blood.According to myLAB Box's website, the At Home Inflammation Test reports high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) and vitamin D from a finger-prick blood sample. The sample is processed in a CLIA and CAP accredited laboratory, and hs-CRP is quantified in milligrams per liter, including the low levels a standard CRP assay cannot resolve. Our explainer on hs-CRP versus standard CRPcovers that distinction if it is new to you.

Sensa measures CRP in saliva and reports a score.You swab saliva into the vial, the reagent inside changes color in response to the CRP present, and you scan the vial with the Sensa app. The app converts the color change into an inflammation score from 0 to 100 and places it in one of three tiers: In range, Worth watching, or Time to reset. This score is not an hs-CRP value. Saliva CRP and blood hs-CRP are different fluids on different scales, and a Sensa score should never be read against blood reference ranges. Sensa is a general wellness product, and its value is in repeated readings that show direction over time.

What that means in practice.A lab hs-CRP value speaks the language your doctor uses. A Sensa score speaks the language of trends: is this week higher or lower than last week, and what changed? Neither replaces the other, which is why this article treats them as two tools rather than two versions of one.

The Experience, Step by Step

myLAB Box, from order to result.You order online and the kit arrives with a lancet, a collection card, and a pre-addressed return envelope with postage paid, according to the product page. You register the kit, prick a fingertip, fill the collection spots, let the card dry, and mail it back. The lab runs the sample, and results post to your secure online account. myLAB Box says that if a result comes back positive, a free consultation with a provider licensed in your state is included. The product page notes the test is temporarily unavailable in New York, and that FSA and HSA cards are accepted.

Sensa, from box to score.You open the kit, swab saliva into the vial, give the color a few minutes to develop, and scan the vial with the Sensa app. In 15 to 30 minutes you have a score and a tier, saved automatically to your history. There is no card to dry, nothing to mail, and no waiting for an email. Kits ship free, there is no subscription, and a multi-pack on the shelf means your next test is always within reach.

Time to Result

myLAB Box runs on shipping time plus lab time.The kit has to reach you, your sample has to travel back, and myLAB Box says results take between two and five days after the lab receives the sample. For a first order, plan on roughly one to two weeks from click to result. Each test is a fresh kit, so the cycle repeats, though keeping a kit on hand removes the first leg.

Sensa takes 15 to 30 minutes.Once the kit is in your hands, the result arrives in the time it takes to make breakfast. Whether that matters depends on your question. If you want to see how a rough week of sleep showed up in your body, a two-week lag blurs the answer. If you need one solid number for an annual physical, the wait is irrelevant.

myLAB Box lists the test at $99.That is the price shown on the product page on November 3, 2026. Shipping is free on orders over $150, so a single kit may carry a shipping charge at checkout; we did not see the rate on the product page itself.

Sensa is $39 for a single kit, $79 for a three-pack, and $103 for a four-pack.That is about $26 per test in either pack, with free shipping and no subscription. Here is what the two cost over a year at several testing frequencies, using myLAB Box's list price and Sensa four-packs.

What You Get With the Result

myLAB Box delivers two lab values and a conditional consult.Your report shows hs-CRP and vitamin D from an accredited lab. If a result is positive, myLAB Box says a free consultation with a licensed provider is included. The vitamin D value is a genuine bonus: it is a common blood test that people often pay for separately, and it rides along in the same finger prick. myLAB Box's own disclaimer says its tests are not intended to diagnose, treat, or cure disease and that results are for educational purposes.

Sensa delivers a score, a tier, and a history.Each reading is a 0 to 100 score labeled In range, Worth watching, or Time to reset, time-stamped and stored in the app alongside every earlier reading. The history is the point. One score tells you little. A line of scores across the weeks you changed your sleep, cut back on alcohol, or started training again tells you something you can act on. Sensa does not diagnose anything, and the app says so.

The honest framing.A lab value tells you where you stood against a population range on one day. A trend tells you which direction you are moving. Most people benefit from knowing both, and most people do not need the lab version very often.

When to Pick Which

Pick myLAB Box when the number has clinical stakes.If your doctor asked for an hs-CRP, if you are following up on a previous lab result, or if you want a value to bring to an appointment, you need a laboratory result. myLAB Box gives you one without leaving home, adds vitamin D at no extra cost, and includes a provider consult if something comes back positive.

Pick Sensa when you want to watch the trend.If you are changing your diet, sleep, training, or stress load and want to see whether it shows up in your body, you need frequent, cheap, needle-free readings. Testing every other week with Sensa costs less than a single myLAB Box kit a month and gives you far more points on the chart.

Use both when you want anchor and direction.Take a lab hs-CRP once or twice a year as an anchor and use Sensa in the months between. Because the two live on different scales, compare direction rather than numbers. If your Sensa trend has drifted toward Worth watching and your next lab value also rose, the two are telling the same story in different languages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sensa an alternative to myLAB Box?

For trend tracking, yes. Sensa gives you a saliva CRP score at home in 15 to 30 minutes for about $26 to $39 per test, which makes frequent testing practical in a way a $99 mail-in kit does not. For a laboratory hs-CRP value, no. Sensa does not produce a clinical number, so if your doctor wants an hs-CRP result, myLAB Box or another lab-based test is the right tool.

What does the myLAB Box inflammation test measure?

High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) and vitamin D, from a finger-prick blood sample processed in a CLIA and CAP accredited laboratory, according to myLAB Box's website on November 3, 2026. The kit is $99, results post online two to five days after the lab receives the sample, and a free consultation with a licensed provider is included if a result is positive.

How long does myLAB Box take?

myLAB Box says results are available between two and five days after the lab receives your sample. Add shipping time in both directions, so a first order typically takes one to two weeks from purchase to result. Sensa gives a saliva CRP score in 15 to 30 minutes, though it is a different measurement.

Which is cheaper, Sensa or myLAB Box?

Sensa, per test and per year. Sensa is $39 for a single kit and about $26 per test in a $103 four-pack, while myLAB Box lists its At Home Inflammation Test at $99. Testing monthly for a year costs about $309 with Sensa four-packs versus $1,188 with myLAB Box at list price. They are not the same product, though: myLAB Box's price includes a lab hs-CRP value, a vitamin D value, and a provider consult if needed.

Can a saliva CRP test replace a blood test?

No. Saliva CRP and blood hs-CRP are different fluids measured on different scales, and a Sensa score is not an hs-CRP value. It should not be compared with blood reference ranges or used in place of a test your doctor ordered. Saliva testing is for watching your trend between lab tests; blood testing is for getting a clinical number.
