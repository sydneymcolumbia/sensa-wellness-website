The short answer

A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) and an inflammation monitor measure different things, so one cannot replace the other. A CGM tracks blood glucose minute by minute, showing your metabolic response to meals, exercise, sleep, and stress. Sensa measures C-reactive protein (CRP), the standard marker of systemic inflammation, which no CGM measures. Glucose tells you how you responded to lunch; CRP tells you the inflammatory load your body has been carrying over recent days and weeks. Many people track both for a fuller picture of their health.

The Background

The CGM Boom, and the Signal It Misses

Continuous glucose monitoring has moved from diabetes care into mainstream wellness. Over-the-counter sensors like Abbott's Lingo and Dexcom's Stelo, and programs like Levels built on top of them, let anyone watch their glucose respond to a bowl of pasta or a bad night of sleep in near real time. It is genuinely useful feedback, and it made continuous biomarker tracking a normal consumer habit.

But glucose is one axis of health: the metabolic one. Inflammation is a different axis. It is the immune system's background activity level, and chronically elevated inflammation is linked to cardiovascular risk, mood, recovery, and aging. You can have flat, healthy glucose curves and still carry elevated inflammation from poor sleep, chronic stress, or a smoldering issue you cannot feel.

No CGM measures inflammation. CRP is the marker clinicians actually use for that, and until recently the only way to check it was a blood draw. Sensa makes CRP trackable at home the way a CGM made glucose trackable, just on the cadence that fits inflammation: repeat spot checks over days and weeks rather than a continuous stream.

Different Timescales, Different Questions

Glucose swings in minutes, so it needs continuous measurement. A CGM answers: "How did my body handle that meal, that workout, that night of sleep?"

CRP moves over hours to days and settles into a baseline over weeks. Regular spot checks answer: "Is the way I am living pushing my inflammation up or down?"

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a CGM measure inflammation?

No. A continuous glucose monitor measures glucose in the interstitial fluid under your skin, which reflects your metabolic response to food, activity, and sleep. Inflammation is measured with markers like C-reactive protein, which requires a different kind of test. Sensa measures CRP from saliva at home.

Are glucose and inflammation related?

They influence each other. Chronically elevated glucose and frequent large spikes are associated with higher inflammatory activity, and chronic inflammation can in turn worsen insulin resistance. But the relationship is loose enough that one cannot stand in for the other. Stable glucose does not guarantee low inflammation, which is why they are worth tracking separately.

Why is Sensa not continuous like a CGM?

Because CRP does not need continuous measurement. Glucose swings meaningfully within minutes, so a CGM samples constantly. CRP changes over hours to days and forms a baseline over weeks, so regular spot checks capture the trend that matters without wearing a sensor. The cadence of the tool matches the biology of the marker.

Should I use a CGM and Sensa together?

If you want a fuller picture, they pair well. A CGM shows the short-term metabolic effects of your choices, and Sensa shows the longer-term inflammatory effects. The same lifestyle levers, diet quality, sleep, exercise, and stress management, tend to improve both signals, so seeing them move together is powerful feedback.
