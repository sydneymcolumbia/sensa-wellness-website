The Best At-Home CRP Tests in 2026: An Honest Comparison

Four very different products all answer to the phrase "at-home CRP test." Here is how they actually compare on sample type, speed, cost, and what kind of answer each one gives you, including where our own test is not the right choice.

Written by Sydney Murphy, CMO & Digital Products Officer. Reviewed by the Sensa Wellness editorial team. Written to reflect current, publicly available inflammation research.

The short answer

It depends on the question you are asking. If your doctor needs a clinical-grade hs-CRP value, a lab-analyzed blood test such as Labcorp OnDemand ($69, draw at a Labcorp location) or Everlywell's mail-in finger-prick kit ($99, results in a few days) is the right tool. If you want to track your inflammation trend frequently without needles or waiting, Sensa's saliva-based test gives you a reading at home in about 15 minutes, at the lowest per-test cost of the group. Full disclosure: Sensa Wellness publishes this site, so we compare our own product here and we tell you plainly where the others win.

Search for "at-home CRP test" and you will find products that differ far more than their names suggest. Some are true at-home tests. Some are at-home sample collection for a distant lab. One requires you to leave the house entirely. They also differ in what kind of number comes back, how long it takes, and what it costs to test more than once.

One thing to know up front: this comparison lives on the Sensa Wellness site, and Sensa is our product. We think the honest version of this article is more useful to you than a sales page, so the comparison below includes the cases where a competitor is the better choice. First, Know What You Are Buying

Wellness tracking and clinical diagnostics are different products. The three blood-based options return a laboratory hs-CRP or CRP value, the same kind of number your doctor would order, and lab hs-CRP remains the clinical gold standard for measuring low-grade inflammation. Sensa is different by design: it is a general wellness product that measures CRP in saliva and turns it into a 0 to 100 score for tracking your trend over time. It does not diagnose, treat, or prevent disease, and it does not replace clinical testing. If a doctor asks for your hs-CRP, hand them a lab result, not a wellness score.

Standard CRP and hs-CRP are not the same test. High-sensitivity CRP detects the low-grade elevations (below 10 mg/L) that matter for long-term wellness and cardiovascular risk conversations, while standard CRP is better suited to large elevations from infection or injury. If you are choosing a lab test, check which one you are getting. We cover the difference in detail in hsCRP vs standard CRP.

The case for checking inflammation at all has strong clinical backing. As Paul Ridker, MD, director of the Center for Cardiovascular Disease Prevention at Brigham and Women's Hospital, said in a Mass General Brigham press release: "Doctors cannot treat what they don't measure. To provide the best care for our patients, we need universal screening for inflammation, cholesterol, and lipoprotein(a), and we need it now."

Sensa: Best for Frequent, Needle-Free Trend Tracking

What it is. Sensa is a saliva-based lateral flow test. You provide a saliva sample in the vial, scan the cuvette with the Sensa app, and get a 0 to 100 inflammation score in about 15 minutes. No needle, no mailing, no lab queue.

Where it wins. Speed, comfort, and repeatability. Because a single kit starts at $49 and multi-packs bring each test to about $32, testing weekly to see how sleep, diet, training, or stress changes affect your inflammation is actually affordable. That kind of frequency is where trends become visible, and trends are more informative for lifestyle decisions than any single reading. The companion app logs every result automatically.

Where it loses. If you need a clinical hs-CRP value in mg/L for a medical conversation, Sensa is not that. Salivary CRP correlates with systemic inflammation but is not interchangeable with a serum measurement, which is why Sensa reports a wellness score rather than imitating a lab unit. Sensa is honest about being a tracking tool, and you should hold us to that.

LetsGetChecked: Best for a Lab CRP Result With Support

What it is. A mail-in kit: you prick your finger, collect blood into a small vial, and send it to a certified lab. Results arrive online in 2 to 5 business days, and a nurse is available to discuss them.

Where it wins. You get a genuine lab value without visiting a clinic, and the nurse follow-up is a real advantage if you are unsure how to interpret a result.

Where it loses. Cost and cadence. At their typical kit pricing, testing monthly runs well over a thousand dollars a year, and the multi-day turnaround makes tight before-and-after experiments impractical. Finger-prick collection also fails for some users, which means a repeat kit and more waiting.

Everlywell: Best if You Also Want Vitamin D

What it is. A $99 mail-in finger-prick kit that measures hs-CRP and vitamin D together, with physician-reviewed results in a few days.

Where it wins. Two biomarkers from one sample is efficient, and vitamin D status is genuinely relevant to inflammation for many people. The physician review adds a layer of interpretation.

Where it loses. Same trade-offs as any mail-in blood kit: a needle, a wait measured in days, and a price that discourages frequent testing. If you only want CRP, you are paying for a panel you did not ask for.

Labcorp OnDemand: Best Pure Lab Number for the Money

What it is. You buy the $69 hs-CRP test online without a doctor's order, then visit a Labcorp location for a standard venous draw. Results post about a day after your sample reaches the lab.

Where it wins. It is the cheapest route in this comparison to a true clinical hs-CRP value, run on the same equipment your doctor's order would use, with fast turnaround.

Where it loses. Calling it an at-home test is generous: you collect nothing at home. You travel to a draw site during business hours and have blood drawn from a vein. For one careful data point a year alongside a physical, that is fine. For tracking, it is the least convenient option here.

So Which Should You Choose?

Choose a lab test when the number has clinical stakes. A doctor conversation, a cardiovascular risk assessment, or a follow-up on a previously elevated result all call for a lab hs-CRP. Labcorp OnDemand is the value pick if a draw site is convenient; Everlywell or LetsGetChecked if you want true at-home collection.

Choose Sensa when the goal is an ongoing picture. If you want to know what your inflammation is doing week over week, and whether the changes you are making are moving it, frequent needle-free testing at a sustainable cost is the whole game. That is the use case Sensa was built for, and the one where mail-in kits are weakest.

The two approaches combine well. A sensible pattern we see: a lab hs-CRP once or twice a year as a clinical anchor, with Sensa in between to watch the trend. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best at-home CRP test?

It depends on the goal. For a clinical-grade hs-CRP value, a lab-analyzed test is best: Labcorp OnDemand ($69, in-person draw) is the value option and Everlywell ($99, mail-in finger prick) adds vitamin D and physician review. For frequent needle-free trend tracking, Sensa's saliva-based test is the only option in this group with instant results at home and a per-test cost around $32 in multi-packs. Sensa is a wellness tracker, not a diagnostic.

Can you test CRP without a blood draw?

Yes. Sensa measures CRP in saliva using a lateral flow test scanned by a phone app, with a result in about 15 minutes and no needle. Salivary CRP correlates with systemic inflammation but is not interchangeable with a serum hs-CRP value, so needle-free testing is best used for wellness trend tracking rather than clinical decisions. All other major at-home options use finger-prick or venous blood.

How much does an at-home CRP test cost?

As of August 2026: Sensa starts at $49 for a single kit with multi-packs at about $32 per test, Labcorp OnDemand's hs-CRP test is $69 plus a visit to a draw site, Everlywell's combined vitamin D and inflammation kit is $99, and LetsGetChecked's blood test kits generally run $89 to $119. Mail-in kits also carry a hidden cost in time, with results taking two to five business days after the lab receives your sample.

Are at-home CRP tests accurate?

Mail-in kits analyzed by certified labs produce genuine clinical hs-CRP values, provided the finger-prick sample is collected properly. Saliva-based testing like Sensa is a different measurement: it reflects CRP in saliva and reports a wellness score for trend tracking, which is useful for watching change over time but is not a substitute for a lab value. For any single high-stakes number, use a lab test and discuss it with a clinician.

How often should you test CRP?

For clinical purposes, once or twice a year alongside routine blood work is typical unless a doctor advises otherwise. For wellness tracking, weekly testing at a consistent time of day builds a meaningful trend line, which is the pattern affordable repeat testing makes practical. Single readings are noisy in any test format because CRP responds to infections, injuries, poor sleep, and stress.
