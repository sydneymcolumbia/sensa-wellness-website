The short answer

Mail-in test kits and Sensa both let you check inflammation without a clinic visit, but they work very differently. A mail-in kit sends a finger-prick blood sample to a certified lab and returns a broad panel of results in several days. Sensa is a reusable device that measures CRP from saliva in minutes, so you can test frequently and watch your inflammation trend change week to week. Mail-in kits suit occasional broad snapshots; Sensa suits ongoing CRP monitoring.

The Background

Two Generations of At-Home Testing

Mail-in testing services such as Everlywell and LetsGetChecked were the first wave of at-home health testing. You order a kit, collect a finger-prick blood sample at your kitchen table, mail it to a certified laboratory, and receive results through an online portal a few days later. For a broad panel of biomarkers checked once or twice a year, this model works well.

The trade-off is the loop: every test means a new kit, a new blood sample, shipping in both directions, and lab queue time. That makes frequent testing expensive and slow, which is exactly what you need if your goal is to see how diet, sleep, exercise, or stress are moving your inflammation over time.

Sensa closes that loop. The measurement happens on the device in your bathroom, from saliva, with a result in the app in minutes. It focuses on one high-value marker, CRP, and makes it practical to check as often as you want.

Why CRP?

C-reactive protein is one of the most widely used markers of systemic inflammation. It responds within hours to infection, injury, poor sleep, and chronic stress, which makes it an unusually good candidate for trend tracking.

A single CRP reading is a snapshot. A series of readings over weeks tells you whether your baseline is drifting up or coming down, and that is where frequent at-home testing shines.

Reference Ranges

What Your CRP Number Means

High-sensitivity CRP (hsCRP) is measured in milligrams per liter (mg/L). These widely referenced cardiovascular risk ranges are educational and not a substitute for a healthcare provider's interpretation.

hsCRP level (mg/L) Category What it typically reflects

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Sensa different from Everlywell or LetsGetChecked?

Mail-in services like Everlywell and LetsGetChecked have you collect a finger-prick blood sample at home, mail it to a certified lab, and wait several days for results. Sensa is a reusable device that measures CRP from a saliva sample and shows your result in the app within minutes, so you can test frequently and track a trend instead of getting occasional snapshots.

Do mail-in kits test for inflammation?

Some mail-in panels include hsCRP alongside other markers such as cholesterol. That reading is processed at a clinical lab and is useful as a periodic snapshot. What mail-in kits are not built for is frequent testing, because each test requires a new kit, a new blood sample, and days of turnaround.

Is a mail-in lab result more accurate than Sensa?

Mail-in samples are analyzed at certified clinical laboratories, which is the right choice when a single precise measurement matters. Sensa is a general wellness product designed for a different job: convenient, repeatable readings that reveal your inflammation trend over time. Many people use an occasional lab panel for depth and Sensa for frequency.

Which should I buy if I only pick one?

It depends on your goal. If you want a broad once-a-year overview of many biomarkers, a mail-in panel covers more ground. If your goal is understanding and improving your inflammation specifically, frequent CRP readings from Sensa will tell you far more than one or two isolated lab values, because you can see how your habits actually move the number.
