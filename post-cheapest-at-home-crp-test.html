What Is the Cheapest At-Home CRP Test? Price per Result Compared

The honest answer has two halves, because the cheapest way to watch a trend and the cheapest way to get a laboratory number are two different purchases. Here is the price per result for every option we could price.

Written by Sydney Murphy, CMO & Digital Products Officer. Reviewed by the Sensa Wellness editorial team. Competitor prices last verified on the sellers' websites on November 3, 2026; we were unable to re-check them on November 4, 2026, so every competitor figure below carries that date.

The short answer

For the lowest cost per result, Sensa is the cheapest at-home CRP test we are aware of: $39 for a single saliva kit, $79 for a three-pack (about $26 per test) and $103 for a four-pack (about $26 per test), with free shipping in the United States and no subscription. If what you actually need is a laboratory hs-CRP value to show a doctor, Sensa is the wrong purchase and the cheapest route is a draw-site test: Labcorp OnDemand listed hs-CRP at $69 and Quest Health at $65 plus a $6 physician fee when last verified on November 3, 2026, both cheaper than the $99 mail-in finger-prick kits from Everlywell and myLAB Box. It is a general wellness product for tracking your own trend over time, not a diagnostic test.

The Cheapest Option Depends on What You Get Back

There are two products hiding under the phrase "at-home CRP test," and they are priced on different logic. The first is at-home sample collection for a laboratory test. You prick a finger or visit a draw site, a lab runs the sample, and you get back an hs-CRP value in milligrams per liter, the kind of number a clinician can read against a reference range. You are paying for a laboratory, a courier and often a physician review, which is why the floor sits near $65. The second is an actual at-home test, where the reading happens in your kitchen. There is no lab in the chain, so the cost per result can fall to roughly the price of a nice lunch.

That difference is also why the two cannot be price-compared like two brands of the same thing. A Sensa reading is a 0 to 100 wellness score in one of three tiers, In range, Worth watching, or Time to reset. It is not an hs-CRP value, it sits on a different scale in a different fluid, and it should never be read against blood reference ranges. If you came here looking for the cheapest clinical number, skip to the draw-site section. If you came here because you want to test often enough to see a trend and $99 a time makes that impossible, read on. Our explainer on hs-CRP versus standard CRP covers the assay distinction, and what a saliva CRP test is covers ours.

Cheapest If You Need a Laboratory hs-CRP Number

Buy from a draw-site lab, not a mail-in kit. This is the part where a competitor wins outright. If your doctor asked for an hs-CRP, if you are following up on a prior result, or if you want a figure that goes in your chart, Labcorp OnDemand at $69 and Quest Health at $65 plus a $6 physician fee are both cheaper than the $99 mail-in kits, and a venous draw at a nearby location is usually faster than mailing a dried sample across the country. At about $26, testing every other week costs less across a year than a dozen mail-in kits, and the app keeps every reading time-stamped in one history so the trend draws itself.

Watch the Costs That Are Not on the Price Tag

The sticker price is not always the checkout total. Four things move the real number. Shipping: myLAB Box ships free on orders over $150, so a single $99 kit may pick up a shipping charge, and Sensa ships free on every order. Service fees: Quest Health's $65 becomes $71 once the $6 physician fee is added, which is why it sits above Labcorp's flat $69 in the table despite the lower headline. Subscriptions: a membership like COR One's is a recurring commitment, and $29 per month billed annually means you pay for twelve months whether you test twelve times or twice. Trips: a draw-site test is cheap on paper and costs you an errand, which is free if the lab is on your commute and expensive if it is not.

Repeat purchases are the cost most people underestimate. Every mail-in kit is a fresh kit, so the price you paid once is the price you pay each time. Here is what a year of testing costs at four frequencies, using the prices above and Sensa four-packs.

Why the Cheapest Test Is Sometimes the Wrong One

Price should be the last filter, not the first. Work out what you want the result to do before you compare numbers. If a clinician is going to read it, you need a laboratory value and the cheapest one is a draw-site test at $69 to $71. If you are curious whether cutting alcohol or fixing your sleep shows up in your body, you need many readings rather than one good one, and a $99 kit is the expensive way to get a thin answer. If you want both, a lot of people use a lab test once or twice a year as an anchor and Sensa in the weeks between, comparing direction rather than numbers, since the two scales do not convert.
