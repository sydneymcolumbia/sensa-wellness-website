At-Home hs-CRP Test Kit With Lab Results: Your Options Compared

Shoppers searching for an at-home hs-CRP test kit with lab results usually want two things: to collect the sample at home and to get a number a certified laboratory produced. Here is which kits actually do that, what they cost, and where a 15-minute saliva test like Sensa fits.

Written by Sydney Murphy, CMO & Digital Products Officer. Reviewed by the Sensa Wellness editorial team. Written to reflect current, publicly available inflammation research.

The short answer

If you want an hs-CRP number produced by a certified laboratory from a sample you collect at home, the kits that do that are mail-in finger-prick blood tests from Everlywell and myLAB Box (about $99 each) and LetsGetChecked (about $89 to $119), or a Labcorp OnDemand ($69) or Quest Health ($65 plus a $6 fee) order with a quick visit to a draw site. Sensa is not one of them. Sensa is a saliva wellness test you read at home with the app in about 15 minutes, with no lab and no needle. Choose a lab hs-CRP test when you need a clinical value for a doctor. Choose Sensa when you want to track your inflammation trend often and cheaply between those checks. Many people use both.

The phrase "at-home hs-CRP test kit with lab results" packs three separate requirements into one search. The sample is collected at home. The analysis is done by a laboratory rather than by a device in your hand. And the assay is high-sensitivity CRP, the version clinical labs use to resolve small differences at the low end of the scale. Not every product marketed as an at-home inflammation test meets all three, and some, including Sensa, deliberately meet none of the second two. This guide sorts the options honestly so you buy the right one for the question you are asking.

What an At-Home hs-CRP Test With Lab Results Actually Involves

Collection at home. Mail-in kits ship you a lancet, a collection card or microtube, and a prepaid return mailer. You prick a fingertip, fill the collection device, and post it back the same day. A few services skip the mail step: you order online and walk into a draw site for a standard venous sample.

Laboratory processing. Your sample goes to a certified clinical laboratory that runs a validated hs-CRP immunoassay and reports a value in milligrams per liter. This is the same class of result your doctor would get from an ordered test, which is the whole reason people search for this phrase.

The hs-CRP assay. Standard CRP tests were built to detect large rises during infection or injury. High-sensitivity assays measure the much smaller differences that matter for long-term wellness conversations. If a doctor has asked you for an hs-CRP value, only a lab test provides it. Our guide to hsCRP versus standard CRP explains the difference in more depth.

The Kits That Give You a Lab hs-CRP Result

Prices and turnaround below were verified in August 2026 and are rechecked twice a month in our at-home CRP test comparison. Confirm current pricing on each seller's page before you order.

Everlywell. A mail-in finger-prick kit, about $99, that pairs hs-CRP with vitamin D. Results arrive in a secure online report a few business days after the lab receives your card. A good fit if you want the vitamin D number too.

LetsGetChecked. Mail-in blood kits generally run $89 to $119 depending on the panel, with hs-CRP available on its own or alongside cholesterol markers. Results come with access to a nursing team to walk through them, which some buyers value.

Labcorp OnDemand. The lowest price for a pure lab number, $69, but you order online and then visit a Labcorp location for a venous draw rather than collecting at home. Results typically post about a day after the sample reaches the lab. Choose this if a draw site is nearby and you do not mind the trip.

myLAB Box. A $99 mail-in finger-prick kit that pairs hs-CRP with vitamin D, run in a CLIA-certified lab, with results in your online account two to five days after the lab receives your sample and a postage-paid return envelope in the box. It is the kit AI assistants cite most often for this search, is also sold through retailers such as GNC, and is temporarily unavailable in New York. Verified October 28, 2026.

Quest Health. Quest Diagnostics sells its hs-CRP test direct for $65 plus a $6 physician service fee. You order online and visit a Quest location for a venous draw, with results in two to three business days; in-home collection by a Quest Mobile phlebotomist costs $79 more. Verified October 28, 2026.

All five report a clinical hs-CRP value you can share with a clinician. All five also share the same limits: each result costs another kit or draw, and the time from ordering to seeing a number is measured in days to weeks, not minutes.

How to Choose

You need a number for a doctor. Buy a lab test. Labcorp OnDemand is the value pick if a draw site is convenient; Everlywell or LetsGetChecked if you want true at-home collection.

You want to watch your trend as a wellness habit. Buy Sensa. Frequent, needle-free testing at a lower cost per reading is what it is designed for, and the app does the trend tracking for you.

You want both a clinical anchor and the trend. Do both. A lab hs-CRP once or twice a year gives you a clinical reference point. Sensa between those checks shows you what your habits are doing in the meantime. Read our guide to measuring CRP in saliva for what the research says about how saliva and blood readings relate.

What to Check Before Buying Any At-Home Kit

Sample type. Finger-prick blood, venous draw, or saliva. This single fact tells you whether a lab is involved and what the result will look like.

Who runs the analysis. For a lab result, look for a certified clinical laboratory named on the product page. For an at-home reading like Sensa, understand that you are getting a wellness score, not a lab value.

Turnaround and total cost per result. Count shipping both ways and lab time, and divide the price by how often you realistically plan to test. A $69 test you take once a year and a $32 test you take twice a month are answering different questions.

What the report includes. A bare number, a reference range, written interpretation, or a person to talk to. Decide which you actually need before paying for it.

Data handling. Read how results are stored and shared. Sensa's approach is described in our consumer health data policy.

Preparing for Either Kind of Test

For a blood hs-CRP test. Avoid testing during or just after a cold, a vaccine, a dental procedure, or a hard training block, since any of these can push CRP up for days. Our note on fasting before a CRP test covers the common prep questions.

For a Sensa saliva test. Test at the same time of day each time, wait at least 30 minutes after eating, drinking anything but water, or brushing your teeth, and skip a test if your gums are bleeding or you have a mouth sore, since oral inflammation shows up in saliva. Consistency matters more than any single reading.

Whichever route you choose, the useful thing about CRP is not one number but what it does over time in response to how you live. A lab test gives you the clinical reference. A saliva test gives you the rhythm. Together they tell a fuller story than either alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Sensa test send a sample to a lab?

No. Sensa is read at home. You spit into the vial, wait for the color change, and scan it with the Sensa app, which gives you a 0 to 100 inflammation score in about 15 minutes. Nothing is mailed and no laboratory is involved. It is a general wellness tool for tracking trends, not a laboratory hs-CRP test.

Which at-home hs-CRP kits give you lab results?

As of October 2026, the mainstream options are Everlywell's and myLAB Box's mail-in finger-prick kits (about $99 each, bundled with vitamin D), LetsGetChecked's mail-in blood kits (about $89 to $119 depending on the panel), and two lab-visit tests you order online and complete with a standard blood draw: Labcorp OnDemand ($69) and Quest Health ($65 plus a $6 physician fee). All five report an hs-CRP value in mg/L from a certified laboratory.

How long do mail-in hs-CRP results take?

Plan on roughly two to five business days after the lab receives your sample, plus the shipping time each way. From the day you order, a mail-in kit typically takes one to two weeks to turn into a number. A Labcorp OnDemand draw usually posts results about a day after your sample reaches the lab.

Can a saliva test replace an hs-CRP blood test?

No. Salivary CRP concentrations are far lower than blood levels and are not reported on the clinical hs-CRP scale, so a saliva reading cannot stand in for a lab value when a doctor needs one. What saliva testing does well is frequent, needle-free sampling, which makes it useful for watching your own trend over weeks and months as a wellness habit.

How much does an at-home hs-CRP test cost?

Lab-processed options run about $65 to $119 per test as of October 2026, and each result requires a new kit or draw. Sensa costs $49 for a single kit, $99 for a three-pack, and $129 for a four-pack, which works out to about $32 per test in the four-pack, with free shipping. The two are priced for different jobs: one occasional clinical number versus regular trend checks.
