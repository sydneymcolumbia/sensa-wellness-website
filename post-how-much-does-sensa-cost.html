How Much Does the Sensa Wellness Test Cost?

The full price list, what each pack works out to per test, what is included, and the one situation where spending your money somewhere else makes more sense.

Written by Sydney Murphy, CMO & Digital Products Officer. Reviewed by the Sensa Wellness editorial team. Sensa prices verified against sensawellness.org on November 5, 2026. Competitor prices last verified on the sellers' websites on November 3, 2026; we were unable to re-check them on November 5, 2026, so every competitor figure below carries that earlier date.

The short answer

Sensa is $39 for a single at-home test kit, $79 for a three-pack (about $26 per test) and $103 for a four-pack (about $26 per test), with free shipping inside the United States and no subscription. That price covers everything you need: the kit, the app and the shipping, with no lab fee, no device to buy and nothing recurring. Sensa is a general wellness kit that reads CRP from saliva at home and gives you a 0 to 100 wellness score in about 15 minutes with no lab and no needle, for tracking your own trend over time rather than diagnosing anything.

This article lives on the Sensa Wellness site, so treat it as the seller answering a pricing question rather than a neutral review. What we can do is show the arithmetic rather than hiding it behind a "from" price, and say plainly where a different purchase would serve you better. The prices below were checked against our own order pages on November 5, 2026.

What the Price Includes

You are buying kits and the app, and that is the whole bill. A Sensa order includes the single-use test kits themselves, access to the Sensa app that reads them, your full result history, and shipping. There is nothing else to pay for later. In particular there is no reader, dock or hardware to buy, because the app uses your phone's camera to read the vial. If you want the mechanics of that, our explainer on measuring CRP in saliva covers what the test is doing and what the research behind it does and does not support.

There is no subscription, and that is deliberate. Sensa is a one-time purchase. No plan starts after your first test, nothing auto-renews and nothing stops working if you do not buy again. This is a real difference from the inflammation products sold on a membership, and it changes the comparison once you work out an annual figure rather than a sticker price.

There is no lab fee, because there is no lab. With a mail-in kit, the price covers laboratory processing, return postage and a review of your result, which is a genuine part of what those kits cost. Sensa has none of those costs because you read the test yourself, which is why a Sensa test lands near $26 rather than near $99. It is also why a Sensa result is a wellness score rather than a laboratory value.

What you need that is not in the box. A smartphone to run the Sensa app, and nothing else. The kits are shelf-stable at room temperature, so there is no refrigeration to arrange, and there are no batteries to replace or charge. Each kit holds what one test needs, so a four-pack is simply four tests sitting on a shelf until you use them.

Which Pack Is Actually Worth It

Buy the single kit to find out whether you will use it. At $39, one kit answers the only question that matters before you spend more: will you actually run the test and look at the result? One score on its own tells you very little, so treat the single kit as a trial of the habit rather than as a measurement that will mean much by itself.

The three-pack is the first point where the trend makes sense. At $79 for three tests, about $26 each, you save $38 against buying three single kits. Three readings is the minimum that shows direction: a baseline, a test after you change something, and a third to tell you whether the change held or the second reading was noise. If you are testing to see whether a new sleep schedule or a diet change registers, this is the pack to buy.

The four-pack is the cheapest per test and the easiest to keep going. At $103 for four tests, about $26 each, you save $53 against four single kits, which is about 34 percent off the single-kit price. It is also the pack that suits two people in a household testing twice each, or one person testing quarterly for a year. If you already know you want to keep tracking, this is the one that costs least per result.

How often should you test? That decides your real annual cost more than the pack you pick, and it is a genuinely open question rather than something we want to oversell. Our guide to how often to test inflammation works through the trade-off between enough readings to see a trend and spending money on readings that tell you nothing new.

When Sensa Is Not Worth the Money

If you need a laboratory number, Sensa is the wrong purchase at any price. A Sensa score is a 0 to 100 wellness score in one of three tiers, In range, Worth watching or Time to reset. It is not an hs-CRP value, it is not measured in milligrams per liter, and it should never be read against blood reference ranges. So if your doctor asked for an hs-CRP, or you want a number to take to an appointment, spending $39 on a Sensa kit buys something that cannot answer the question. A draw-site test is then both the right tool and the cheaper one: Quest Health listed hs-CRP at $65 plus a $6 physician fee and Labcorp OnDemand at $69 when last verified on November 3, 2026, which is less than the $99 mail-in kits. Our guide to at-home hs-CRP kits with lab results covers those routes, and what a CRP test costs covers the clinical side including insurance.

If you will only ever test once, the cost per useful answer is poor. Sensa's value comes from repeated readings. One score, with nothing to compare it against, is close to meaningless, which is a strange thing for a seller to write but it is true. If you want a single snapshot and nothing more, buy a lab test and get a number that means something on its own.

If a membership would genuinely cost you less, take it. At $29 per month billed annually, COR One works out to about $348 for a year of unlimited readings when last verified on November 3, 2026. If you intend to test very frequently, that can beat buying Sensa packs, with the caveats that it measures ESR rather than CRP and still involves a lancet. Our roundup of at-home CRP tests and our guide to testing inflammation at home lay out the full set of options.

The Short Version on Price

Sensa costs $39, $79 or $103 depending on how many tests you want, which is $39 or about $26 per test, with free shipping and nothing recurring. That makes it the least expensive way we know of to take an inflammation reading at home, and the only one with no needle. It is still a wellness tracking tool rather than a clinical test, and no price changes that. Buy it to watch your own trend. Buy a lab test when you need a number.
