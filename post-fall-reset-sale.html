Why Fall Is the Right Season for a Baseline

Your routine is changing right now. The end of summer is one of the biggest lifestyle shifts of the year. Vacations end, schedules tighten, workouts move indoors, days get shorter, and sleep patterns drift. All of those inputs influence inflammation, and most people have no idea which direction their body is trending when the season turns. A baseline reading now gives every later reading context.

Cold and flu season is coming. Respiratory infections reliably push CRP up while your immune system does its work, which is exactly why it helps to know your healthy baseline before the sniffles arrive. If you know you normally sit near 1 mg/L, a temporary spike during a cold makes sense, and you can watch it settle back down as you recover. We wrote about this in detail in Can a Cold or Flu Raise Your CRP?

The holidays are the hardest test of the year. From late November through New Year's, sleep, alcohol, sugar, stress, and travel all pile up at once. People who start tracking in September and October head into that gauntlet with two months of trend data, so they can actually see what the season does to their body and course-correct in real time instead of guessing in January.

What to Do With Your Fall Baseline

Start with one honest reading. Test on a normal day, not right after a hard workout or while fighting a bug, and log it. That number is your reference point. If you are new to CRP, our guide to understanding your CRP numbers explains what the ranges mean.

Then retest on a rhythm. Every couple of weeks is plenty for most people. The value is not any single reading but the line they draw together: is your baseline holding steady, creeping up as the semester or quarter gets stressful, or coming down as your new routine kicks in? That is the feedback loop that makes lifestyle changes stick.

And bring the trend to your next checkup. A series of readings across the fall is a far richer conversation starter with your healthcare provider than a single annual lab value.
